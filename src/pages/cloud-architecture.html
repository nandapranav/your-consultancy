10 / THE PLATFORM LAYER

From diagram to running platform.

An architecture diagram is a hypothesis. A landing zone, a pipeline, and a cost guardrail are what actually run in production.

The distance between a target architecture and a real cloud platform is filled with account structures, networking, identity, infrastructure-as-code, service catalogs, and the guardrails that keep teams moving fast without creating a compliance or cost incident.

We help build that platform layer: landing zones, multi-account strategy, network topology, IAM boundaries, CI/CD, and the paved paths that let application teams ship without becoming cloud experts themselves.

Account structure, networking, and identity boundaries designed for isolation, blast-radius control, and growth.

02 / GUARDRAILS

03 / PAVED PATHS

Templates, service catalogs, and CI/CD that make the secure, compliant way the easy way.

04 / OPERATIONS

Observability, on-call ownership, and runbooks so platform incidents have a clear owner and a clear fix.

11 / HOW WE WORK

Workload first. Technology second.

The right architecture depends on what actually has to run, not on which platform is fashionable this year.

We start with workloads: their latency, availability, data gravity, compliance, and cost profile. That determines whether a service belongs on managed compute, containers, serverless, or something more specialized—rather than forcing every workload into one pattern.

From there we design for the constraints that are actually binding: existing investments, team skill, regulatory requirements, and the realistic pace of change an organization can absorb.

A / PRINCIPLE

Fit the workload

Compute, storage, and networking choices follow requirements—not a platform preference decided in advance.

B / PRINCIPLE

Design for failure

Every component assumes something upstream will eventually fail, and is built to degrade gracefully.

C / PRINCIPLE

Make it operable

Architecture isn't done until someone on the client's team can run, monitor, and change it confidently.

12 / REFERENCE SYSTEM

The layers of a cloud platform.

A dependable cloud platform is a stack of layers, each with a clear owner and a clear responsibility.

We separate foundations from workloads from operations so a change in one layer—a new region, a new compliance requirement, a new workload pattern—doesn't force a rewrite of the others.

01

Foundations

Accounts, networking, identity, and the org-level guardrails everything else is built on.

02

Platform

Infrastructure-as-code, CI/CD, and the service catalog that standardizes how workloads get deployed.

03

Workloads

Compute, data, and integration patterns matched to the actual requirements of each system.

04

Resilience

High availability, disaster recovery, and failure-mode design tested against real scenarios.

05

Operations & FinOps

Observability, incident response, and cost visibility that keep the platform healthy and affordable.

14 / COMMON QUESTIONS

Built around real work.

Is this strategy or engineering?

Both, when both are needed. We use strategy to determine what should happen and engineering depth to make sure the recommendation can survive contact with the real environment.

Do you work with existing teams?

Yes. The strongest engagements pair Fifth Cursor expertise with the people who own the systems and outcomes. We aim to transfer context and capability rather than create permanent dependency.

How do you choose technology?

We start with workload requirements, constraints, risk, economics, team capability, and existing investments. Tools are selected after the problem is understood.

How do projects stay measurable?

We define success before implementation and track operational, adoption, quality, financial, and business indicators throughout delivery.

What happens after the roadmap?

Where useful, we continue into implementation, architecture, platform work, pilots, or delivery support. The goal is momentum—not a binder that gets forgotten.
